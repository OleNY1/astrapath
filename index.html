The problem

Getting to the building is solved.

The minutes inside it are not.

We talked with students and families and read Reddit threads from people trying to find rooms. The same three problems kept coming up.

01

Maps stop at the front door.

The map’s job ends at the building. Inside, people fall back on signs, room numbers, and following the crowd.

“Google Maps had done its job by getting me to Hamilton.”

A Columbia first-year who arrived about four minutes late to a discussion section
02

“Floor four” isn’t a direction.

Most people can find the floor. They lose the minutes choosing the hallway, then walking back from a dead end.

“If the inside directions were vague, like ‘go to floor four,’ I was already doing that.”

The same first-year
03

Wrong turns break trust.

Asked what would worry them, four of the six people we interviewed about indoor directions named accuracy first.

“If it tells me to turn left and there’s actually no hallway there, I’m going to stop trusting it.”

A student we interviewed

How it works

From the door to the room, in three steps.

  1. 01

    Open it where you are

    From a link in your calendar, a QR code at the entrance, the iPhone app, or your AI assistant.

  2. 02

    Say where you’re going

    Type a room number, or ask in your own words. “Somewhere quiet to wait before my 2 pm in Mudd.”

  3. 03

    Follow what you’ll actually see

    Every step names the hallway, the door, or the elevator and the floors it reaches.

AI understands the question. The map gives the answer.

Routes are computed from a model of the building, step by step. AI never invents a hallway.

What AI does

  • Works out what you mean by “somewhere to print” or “the nearest restroom”
  • Suggests places that fit what you asked for
  • Answers follow-up questions in plain words

What the map does

  • Finds the route through the building’s own hallways, doors, stairs, and elevators
  • Uses only connections that exist in the map
  • Keeps step-free routes and door rules exact

Who it’s for

One building, a different route for each person.

01

First visit, ten minutes to spare

Which hallway, which stairwell, and which elevator actually goes to your floor.

02

Visiting, not working there

The entrance visitors use and where to check in. Where a building shares its access rules, the route uses only doors open to you.

03

Need the step-free way

Ramps and elevators, with the elevator named and the floors it reaches. Step-free is a route of its own, not a hidden setting.
